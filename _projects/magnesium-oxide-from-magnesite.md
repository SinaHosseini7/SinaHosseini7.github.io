## Motivation

Magnesite ore is mostly magnesium carbonate, but natural ore also carries other minerals and impurities. To make magnesium oxide from it, the magnesium has to be separated from everything else. My MSc thesis was an experimental study of a wet chemical route that does this in solution. The work broadened my laboratory skills into experimental process engineering.

## Approach

Before leaching, I calcined the ore in a furnace. The route has three chemical stages. First, I leached the ore with sulfuric acid to bring the magnesium into solution as magnesium sulfate. Next, I added sodium hydroxide to precipitate impurities and filtered them out. Finally, I added ammonia to convert the purified magnesium sulfate into magnesium hydroxide, which I then washed several times and calcined in a furnace to obtain magnesium oxide.

I worked in large beakers of about 5 to 7 liters and ran every step myself, from leaching and filtration to precipitation. Removing impurities from the solution was the central practical task. For analysis, I used XRF, XRD, FTIR, and titration.

Alongside the lab work, I ran a systematic search of the literature and patents. I used Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, ACS Publications, and Google Patents, as well as AI search tools such as Scopus AI and Scinito AI. I collected and sorted more than 500 articles and patents in EndNote. I also managed the experimental budget for the work.

## Results

The results are being prepared for publication, so I do not report them here. What the project produced is a complete laboratory process, from raw ore to magnesium oxide, that I set up and ran alone. The searches also form the basis of review manuscripts now in preparation ([publications](/publications/)).
